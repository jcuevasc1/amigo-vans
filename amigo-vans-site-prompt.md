## WHO THIS IS FOR

Build a website for **Amigo Vans**, a mobile camper van conversion company serving the Phoenix and Tucson metro areas in Arizona. We do not have a shop. We come to the customer's property — ranch, warehouse, garage, acreage — and build the van there. Turnaround is measured in weeks, not months.

Tagline: **"A handshake and a spec sheet."**

Domain: amigovans.com

Bilingual site, English and Spanish, with a language toggle in the header. Both languages fully translated, not a partial machine job. This matters — we serve Phoenix, Tucson, and Nogales, and none of our competitors offer it.

## POSITIONING — READ THIS BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING

We sell **one premium build.** There is no budget tier and no second product line. Everything we install as standard is equal to or better than what the highest-rated builder in the industry ships.

The rugged, off-road, heavy-duty content is **not a separate model.** It is a set of upgrades a customer chooses. The site must still *look* capable of extreme builds or nobody will click those upgrades — so the gallery and hero imagery lean rugged and desert-capable even though there is one tier.

Feature this quote prominently near the top of the homepage, styled as a pull quote with attribution:

> "Our starting model has nothing but the same or better components than the current industry trophy winner. Simple as that."
> — Amigo Vans, Founder

## PAGE STRUCTURE

### 1. Hero

Full-width rugged van image. Headline, the founder quote, the starting price, and **two buttons side by side** — this is critical, the site has two front doors and never a plain contact form on its own:

- **"Build your van"** → goes to the configurator
- **"Talk to a person"** → opens WhatsApp directly

Some buyers want to explore alone. Some want a human immediately. Never force one path.

### 2. We come to you

Its own section. Explain the mobile model plainly: we arrive at the customer's property with tools, components, and crew, and we build on site. No shop queue, no waiting list, no shuttling the van across town. Start dates in about two weeks. Include imagery of a build in progress on open property.

### 3. What comes standard

Present the standard spec as a clean spec table. This is the parity argument — everything here ships on every build.

**Electrical**
- Battle Born 270Ah lithium battery bank
- Victron MultiPlus-II 3000W pure sine inverter-charger
- Victron SmartSolar MPPT controller, 800W solar, expandable to 1,400W
- Victron Orion XS DC-to-DC charging
- Victron Cerbo GX with touchscreen — monitor and control the whole system from your phone
- Shore power inlet
- 110V outlets plus USB-A and USB-C throughout

**Galley**
- 1,800W induction cooktop
- Dometic 12V compressor refrigerator
- Stainless sink with hot *and* cold water
- Microwave
- Filtered drinking tap

**Climate**
- Diesel or gas air heater
- 12V roof air conditioning
- MaxxAir roof fan
- Three-layer insulation and sound dampening

**Water**
- Fresh and grey tanks
- Water heater
- Interior shower
- Toilet

**Structure and interior**
- L-track floor and gear walls — bikes, boards, skis, kayaks
- Aluminum cabinetry
- Armored wheel wells
- Modular seating that converts to sleeping

**Chassis**
- Mercedes Sprinter, Ford Transit, or Ram ProMaster
- Van Compass suspension, wheels, and tires

Add a line at the bottom of the table: *Every component above is standard. Not a package, not an upgrade.*

### 4. Configurator — chassis first

This is the self-serve front door. Build it as a guided flow, **not a shopping cart.** We sell builds, not shipped parts. Nothing is ever checked out or paid for on this site.

**Step one — four large buttons:**
- Mercedes Sprinter
- Ford Transit
- Ram ProMaster
- Don't have a van yet? We can help.

**Step two — options, filtered by chassis.** Only show what fits the chassis they picked. If they picked "Don't have a van yet? We can help," show the universal items only and note that chassis-specific options get discussed on the call.

Do not ask for year, wheelbase, roof height, or drivetrain on the site. Those conversations happen after first contact.

**Step three — summary screen.** Show their chassis, every option they selected, and a rough total range. One button below it:

**"Send this to Amigo Vans on WhatsApp"**

Tapping it opens WhatsApp with the full spec pre-filled as a message. No backend, no form, no inbox, no database. The message is the lead.

### 5. Options catalogue

The deepest options list in the business — deliberately the opposite of competitors who offer one configuration and nothing else. Structure it in two layers, and split every item into one of two clearly labeled groups:

- **Installed on any build** — we stock it, it goes on
- **Sourced and fitted on request** — lead time quoted at contact

Be honest about which is which. Never imply we stock something we don't.

**Layer one — named packages** (bundled, discounted):
- Rugged Terrain Package
- Overland Storage Package
- Off-Grid Power Package
- Desert Climate Package
- Outdoor Living Package

**Layer two — à la carte**, grouped by category:

*Suspension and chassis* — lift kits, upgraded shocks, wheels and tires, skid plates

*Steel and armor (fabricated in-house)* — steel fender flares, front bumper, rear bumper with tire carrier, rock sliders, roof rack, rear gear box, ladder, awning brackets

*Electrical* — additional battery capacity, expanded solar to 1,400W, second alternator charging, additional outlets and circuits, **America and Europe ready external connections** (dual-standard shore power inlet — plug in on either continent without an adapter, for owners who ship or travel their van abroad)

*Climate* — upgraded insulation, secondary heat, window covers and thermal shades

*Water* — expanded tank capacity, exterior shower, water filtration upgrade

*Interior* — cabinetry finishes, upholstery and materials, additional seating, custom layout work

*Exterior and lighting* — light bars, rock lights, awning, roof deck, exterior storage

*Tech and aerial* — rooftop drone dock (weatherproof, self-charging enclosure so the drone is always charged and never has to be unpacked — runs off the van's 12V system, which is only possible on a build with our power spec), satellite internet, exterior camera system, cellular booster, additional monitoring and connectivity gear

**Note on the drone dock:** it is a "sourced and fitted on request" item with a quoted lead time, never shown as in stock. The unit is compact — roughly 28 × 26 × 13 inches closed — so it sits on the roof alongside solar rather than replacing it, but it still has to be planned into the build from the start. Describe it as a self-charging rooftop enclosure. Do not advertise autonomous flight, waypoint missions, or beyond-visual-line-of-sight operation; those require FAA certification the average owner will not hold.

*Outdoor and gear (fits any van, no chassis needed)* — rooftop tent, ground tent, grill and cooking gear, camp furniture, recovery gear, Amigo Vans merchandise

**Pricing display — bands only, never dollar figures:**
- **Included** — comes standard
- **Plus** — modest add
- **Premium** — significant add

### 6. Steel work — fabricated, not bought

Its own section, because it's a real differentiator.

We fabricate our steel in-house rather than buying it off a catalogue — flares, bumpers, racks, sliders, gear boxes. Heavier gauge than most catalogue parts, built to fit the specific gear that specific customer carries, and priced well under retail because we make it ourselves.

**Important accuracy note for whoever writes this copy:** competitors *do* offer fender flares. Do not claim otherwise. The honest and stronger claim is the difference in kind — **theirs are insulated composite flares that add interior width; ours are steel armor that protects the body from rock and tire debris.** Different purpose, different material. Write it that way.

### 7. Build-progress photos

Name this as a feature and give it a section. Customers get regular photo updates through the entire build — not on request, automatically. Nobody in this market sells this, and it's the thing customers of other builders say they wish they'd had.

### 8. Paying for it

Two paths, presented plainly.

**If you already own the van** — you're financing the build itself. Local credit unions, Alliant, LightStream, and SoFi all write this kind of loan.

**If you're buying the van and the build together** — this is RV-style lending, usually longer terms and better rates. Southeast Financial, Good Sam, and Alliant all lend here.

**Required disclaimer, displayed clearly in this section:**

> Amigo Vans is not a lender and not a broker. We receive nothing from any lender we mention. These are starting points for your own research — your terms are between you and them.

### 9. Warranty

**Two years on the build — workmanship and installation. No mileage cap.**

Components carry their own manufacturer warranties, and we administer those claims for you.

And the part that matters: **our warranty service is mobile too.** We come back to you. You don't drive your van across the state and leave it for three weeks.

### 10. What we won't do

A short, honest section. It builds more trust than any claim on the page.

Cover: we don't do half-builds or finish someone else's project; we don't rush structural or electrical work to hit a date; we don't quote a price we haven't verified; we don't work on a property that isn't safe or legal to build on.

Keep the tone plain and confident, not apologetic.

### 11. FAQ — homepage version

Six to eight questions maximum, focused on money and time. These are the questions that stop a sale:

- How much does a build cost?
- How long does it take?
- Do I need to have a van already?
- Where do you build it?
- What if something breaks after?
- Do you finance?

Short answers. Link at the bottom to the full FAQ page.

### 12. Full FAQ — separate page

Everything else, grouped by category: process, components, chassis, warranty, logistics, service area.

Do not add questions about insurance, permits, or vehicle registration. If a customer needs those answers they will ask directly, and we would rather handle it in conversation than publish something we have to walk back.

### 13. Coming soon — Tijuana

A notice section, styled as an announcement rather than a live service area:

**Coming soon to Tijuana — serving San Diego and Los Angeles.**

Same model: we come to your location, with a build facility south of the border.

### 14. Footer

Phone number as tap-to-call, WhatsApp link, service area (Phoenix and Tucson metros), language toggle, and the not-a-lender disclaimer repeated in small text.

## SITEWIDE REQUIREMENTS

**Floating WhatsApp button, bottom-right, on every page.** Always visible, never covers content on mobile. The pre-filled message includes the name of the page the customer was on when they tapped it, so we know what they were looking at.

**Mobile-first.** Most of this traffic is a phone. Design for the phone first and let the desktop layout follow.

**Tap-to-call everywhere** the phone number appears.

**No backend.** No forms that submit anywhere, no database, no email capture, no user accounts. Every path ends in WhatsApp or a phone call.

**Configuration block at the top of the code** holding phone number, WhatsApp number, owner name, and starting price — so these can be edited later without touching anything else.

**Imagery:** hero and gallery lean rugged and desert-capable. Interior shots show the finish quality. Include at least one image of a build happening on open property, since that's the whole model.

**Tone:** direct, confident, plain-spoken. No RV-industry marketing language. Write like the founder talks — a handshake and a spec sheet.
